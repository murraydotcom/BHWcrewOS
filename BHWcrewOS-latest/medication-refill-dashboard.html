CharmHealth prescription workflow

Use only after administrative screening and within your formal training and permissions.

During encounterEncounter prescriptions → Past Rx → choose prior Rx
Between visitsPatient Dashboard → Medications → + Prescriptions → Past Rx (Quick Rx)
Provider reviewPrescriber verifies and signs: drug, strength, form, SIG, quantity, refills, diagnosis, monitoring, pharmacy
TransmissionEligible delegated agent may transmit signed non-controlled Rx; verify Prescriptions → e-Rx Sent

Use Repeat only for an exact documented authorized continuation. Review the most recent prescription date - not just screen position. EPCS controlled substances remain in the authorized prescriber workflow.

Closeout documentation

  • Date/time and patient identifiers
  • Pharmacy and medication / strength
  • Last completed clinical visit and most recent Rx
  • Category / hold, reported symptoms or concerns, and monitoring status
  • Prescriber decision and authorization status
  • e-Rx transmission confirmation
  • Patient notification, follow-up, staff initials, and closure date

Do not independently choose a diagnosis, change dose, substitute brand/generic, or interpret labs or screening scores.

Escalate immediately

Emergency protocol

Chest pain, severe respiratory distress, anaphylaxis, significant bleeding, seizure, severe confusion, or imminent self-harm danger. Do not place in routine refill queue.

Same-day provider

Potentially harmful interruption of insulin, anticoagulants, corticosteroid replacement, antiepileptics, or essential cardiac therapy; serious adverse effect; high-risk psychiatric concern.

Appointment / bridge decision

No completed BHW clinical encounter in 90 days: schedule follow-up and route to prescriber. Staff never independently withhold essential therapy.

90-day standard

A completed BHW in-person or clinical telehealth encounter counts. Canceled/no-show visits, lab-only visits, and administrative contacts do not. Routine preparation requires an active established medication, unchanged directions / strength / formulation, current required monitoring, no new adverse effects or discrepancy, and prescriber approval.

Behavioral health requests

Collect the patient’s own report of adherence, symptom response, adverse effects, sleep/mood changes, missed doses, and other prescriber changes. Route immediately for new/increased suicidal thinking, mania-like symptoms, psychosis, marked agitation, withdrawal symptoms, or significant rash after lamotrigine.

Training scope

  • Generic/brand recognition and controlled-substance red flags
  • Medication-safety screening and behavioral health escalation
  • CharmHealth Past Rx, prescriber signing, and e-Rx status
  • Privacy, role-based privileges, and documented competency
  • Permitted actions under prescriber supervision and applicable Maryland rules

Medical Director sign-off and staff-specific authorization remain required before use as policy.

Medication-specific reminders from the packet

Cardiovascular, blood thinning, and diabetes
  • ACE inhibitors / ARBs may need kidney function and potassium monitoring. Verify ER vs immediate release; do not split or replace combination products.
  • Do not let anticoagulant or essential cardiac prescriptions run out without urgent prescriber review. Warfarin generally needs INR monitoring; DOAC dosing depends on indication and patient factors.
  • No independent insulin-dose changes or injectable-brand substitution. Escalate hypo/hyperglycemia symptoms, acute illness, vomiting/dehydration, or interrupted insulin.
Endocrine, respiratory, and psychiatric
  • Thyroid therapy started or changed may require prescribed testing; central hypothyroidism needs individualized monitoring. Adrenal replacement interruption is urgent.
  • Frequent rescue-inhaler use, new wheeze, or dyspnea prompts clinical review. Montelukast carries a serious neuropsychiatric warning.
  • New/worsening depression, suicidal thoughts, possible mania, significant adverse effects, or missed doses elevate even a green antidepressant to clinician review. Lithium, lamotrigine, antipsychotics, and clozapine have their listed monitoring needs.
Anti-infectives and source notes
  • New infection treatment requires a clinician’s documented assessment and decision. A chronic prophylactic/suppressive plan follows the patient-specific documented plan.
  • Reference notes in source packet: COMAR 10.27.28; CharmHealth prescriptions / e-Rx guidance; FDA clozapine safety communication (2025).
